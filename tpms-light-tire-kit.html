The TPMS light means one or more tires is significantly underinflated — usually 25% below the recommended pressure — not that a tire is about to explode, and not that the sensor is broken. The right response is boring and fast: check all four tires cold, find the low one, top it off to the door-jamb number, and drive a few miles for the light to clear. If the light blinks first, that is a sensor fault, not a pressure reading, and that is a shop visit.

Common TPMS mistakes

  1. Filling to the number on the tire sidewall. That is a maximum, not your car's target. Use the door jamb.
  2. Checking hot after a freeway run. Heat raises the reading and hides the shortfall.
  3. Clearing the light without finding the low tire. The light will come back, because the cause is still there.

Cold mornings trigger TPMS lights in waves: air contracts as temperature drops, so a tire that was fine in summer reads low in winter. That is physics, not a broken car — and it is exactly the morning a trunk kit pays for itself.

Frequently asked questions

What does the TPMS light actually mean?

One or more tires is significantly underinflated — usually about 25% below the recommended pressure. It does not mean a tire is about to explode, and it does not mean the sensor is broken. It means check all four tires cold, find the low one, and top it off.

Why did the light come on if my tires look fine?

A tire a few PSI down does not look flat — it takes a big drop before the eye catches it. That is why the light exists. Check cold with a gauge or the kit's live readout; the number will show what your eyes cannot.

Will the light go off by itself after I add air?

Usually, after a few miles of driving at the correct pressure. If the light blinks first instead of staying solid, that is a sensor fault, not a pressure reading — top off anyway, then book a shop visit for the sensor.

Should I fill my tires to the number on the sidewall?

No. Your car's target lives on the driver's door-jamb sticker, not on the tire itself. The sidewall figure is the tire's maximum, not a target — filling to it over-inflates the tire and wears the center of the tread. The jamb sticker — usually around 32–36 PSI — is the number the car was built for.

Why does the TPMS light come on in cold weather?

Air contracts as temperature drops, roughly 1 PSI for every 10°F. A tire that was fine in summer reads low on the first cold morning, so whole neighborhoods see the light on the same day. Top it off cold and the light clears after a few miles.
