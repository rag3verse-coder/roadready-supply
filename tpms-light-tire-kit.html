TPMS Light On?

TPMS light on — what the warning means, which tire to check first, and how to reset it correctly.

Prices and specs reflect the RoadReady listing as of October 2026. This page reports no hands-on testing and quotes no customer reviews — none exist yet to quote.

The TPMS light means one or more tires is significantly underinflated — usually 25% below the recommended pressure — not that a tire is about to explode, and not that the sensor is broken. The right response is boring and fast: check all four tires cold, find the low one, top it off to the door-jamb number, and drive a few miles for the light to clear. If the light blinks first, that is a sensor fault, not a pressure reading, and that is a shop visit.

Common TPMS mistakes

  1. Filling to the number on the tire sidewall. That is a maximum, not your car's target. Use the door jamb.
  2. Checking hot after a freeway run. Heat raises the reading and hides the shortfall.
  3. Clearing the light without finding the low tire. The light will come back, because the cause is still there.

Cold mornings trigger TPMS lights in waves: air contracts as temperature drops, so a tire that was fine in summer reads low in winter. That is physics, not a broken car — and it is exactly the morning a trunk kit pays for itself.
