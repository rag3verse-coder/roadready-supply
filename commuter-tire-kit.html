Frequently asked questions

How often should a commuter check tire pressure?

Once a week, cold, before the first drive of the day — Monday morning works for most commuters. A tire a few PSI down will not look flat in the driveway, but it rides worse, wears faster, and drinks more fuel all week. A weekly cold check catches it before the dashboard light does.

Can I top off my tires at work or in a parking garage?

Yes. The kit runs on its built-in rechargeable battery, so it needs no outlet and no running engine — set the number, let the auto-stop do the stopping, and drive. If the battery sat dead for months, the 12V car socket runs the same repair.

What pressure should I set the inflator to?

The number on the driver's door-jamb sticker — usually around 32–36 PSI for a commuter car — not the number on the tire sidewall, which is the tire's maximum. If you are unsure, the PSI Finder walks through finding the right range by vehicle type.

Why does the TPMS light come on more in cold weather?

Cold air takes up less space, so pressure falls as the temperature falls — roughly 1 PSI for every 10°F. A commuter who leaves before sunrise feels it first: the tire was at the right number in the afternoon heat and is a few PSI short by morning. Top it off before you drive and the light clears after a few miles.

Will a portable inflator keep up with a daily commute?

For top-offs, yes — a few PSI here and there is exactly what it is for. But if the same tire needs air every few days, that is a slow leak, not a commute problem: top it off to stay safe, then book a shop visit instead of topping off forever.
