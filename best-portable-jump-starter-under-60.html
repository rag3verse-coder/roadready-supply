The 5 specs that actually matter

Peak amps is the number on the box, and it is also the number most likely to mislead you. What decides a dead-battery morning is everything around it:

  1. Engine-size rating, not peak amps. Peak amps are a burst rating — a second of current, not sustained power. The number to shop by is the largest engine the pack is rated to start, gas and diesel separately. A 1,000-amp pack rated for 6.0L gas engines (NOCO GB40) is a more honest buy than a 2,000-amp pack with no engine rating at all.
  2. Charge hold — the trunk test. A jump starter lives in your trunk for months between uses. Lithium packs self-discharge, and a pack that is dead when you need it is a paperweight. NOCO says the GB40 holds about 70% of its charge for up to a year and recommends a top-up every four to six months. Whatever you buy, put a recharge reminder in your calendar.
  3. Safety protections, not just a clamp. Reverse-polarity protection, spark-proof clamps and over-current shutoff are the difference between a jump start and a fried battery or a scare. Every pack in this table claims them; treat their absence in any other listing as a dealbreaker.
  4. The real price, not the coupon price. Budget jump starters are the worst offenders here: a $59.99 list price that only appears after clipping a 50% coupon is not a $59.99 price, and the coupon is gone next week. RoadReady is $59.99 printed plain. NOCO is $99.95 printed plain. If a price needs a code, a membership and a sale to exist, that is not the price.
  5. What the second emergency costs you. A dead battery is one way a car strands you. A flat tire is the other — and a jump starter does nothing for it. That is why the bundle matters more than any single spec on this page: the kit that covers both failures is worth more than the pack with the biggest number.

Before you buy: know your engine

Your engine size is printed in the owner's manual and on the sticker under the hood — every jump-starter rating is written against that number. Diesels need far more starting current than a gas engine of the same size, so check the diesel rating on its own: a gas-only pack is the wrong buy for a diesel.
