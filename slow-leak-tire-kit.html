A slow leak is the most common tire problem and the easiest to ignore: the tire looks fine, the light comes on every few days, you top it off and forget it. That cycle is the warning. A tire that needs air weekly has a reason — a nail, a valve issue, a bead leak, or a puncture that sealed partially. Topping off buys safe miles to a shop; it does not fix the cause, and the longer the cycle runs, the closer it gets to a blowout.

How to handle it

  1. Note the pattern: same tire, how often, how much air.
  2. Top off to the door-jamb number with the cutoff set, never by feel.
  3. Look and listen at the tire while it fills, and check the valve cap and tread for a nail.
  4. Book the shop visit on a schedule, not on the day it finally goes flat. If pressure falls within minutes, stop and call for help.

Frequently asked questions

How do I know if it is a slow leak or just cold weather?

Look at the pattern. Cold weather drops all four tires together, roughly 1 PSI per 10°F. A slow leak hits the same tire again and again while the other three hold. Same tire, every few days, needing air — that is a leak, not the weather.

How long can I keep topping off a slow leak?

Topping off buys safe miles to a shop; it does not fix the cause. Book the shop visit on a schedule — this week, not the day the tire finally goes flat. The longer the cycle runs, the closer it gets to a blowout, and a blowout costs far more than a repair.

What causes a slow leak?

Usually a nail or screw in the tread, a valve that no longer seals, a bead leak where the tire meets the rim, or a puncture that partially sealed itself. All four are shop fixes. The kit's job is to keep you safe on the way there, not to repair the tire.

Can the kit find where the leak is?

No — it tops the tire off. While it fills, look and listen at the tire and check the valve cap and the tread for a nail. That tells you whether it is safe to drive to a shop or whether you should call for help instead.

When does a slow leak become an emergency?

When pressure falls within minutes of topping off, when you can see a cut or a sidewall bulge, or when the tire is visibly flat. Any of those means stop and call for help — do not keep adding air and driving on it.
