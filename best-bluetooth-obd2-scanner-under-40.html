The 5 specs that actually matter

A Bluetooth scanner is a radio with an app attached. Judge the radio, the app, and the bill — in that order:

  1. Which systems it can reach. Every scanner here reads engine codes — the ones behind the check engine light. The split starts one layer down: ABS, airbag, transmission. BlueDriver reaches them across 13 make groups; the OBDLink MX+ reaches them through its own app or third-party apps like FORScan; FIXD reaches none of them at any tier, free or Premium. A scanner that cannot see the ABS module cannot tell you why the ABS light is on. Ask that question of every scanner here, ours included.
  2. The app is the product. The dongle is a radio; the app is the translator, the repair database, the live-data screen. BlueDriver's app carries Repair Reports — verified fixes for your exact year, make and model, ranked by how often each one solved the problem. The OBDLink MX+ ships with a free app and opens the door to Torque and FORScan. The Veepeak ships with no app at all and leans on free third-party apps like Car Scanner. FIXD's free app reads codes and sends maintenance reminders; the features most buyers want — repair-cost estimates, the mechanic hotline, emissions pre-check — sit behind Premium. A great dongle with a bad app is a bad scanner, and that is why RoadReady names its app only after the sample has been tested with it.
  3. What it costs over five years, not on day one. A $39.99 sensor with a $99.99-a-year subscription is a $140 scanner in year one and roughly $540 by year five — counted from FIXD's own pages in October 2026, five years runs $369.94 to $559.94 depending on the sensor price you start from. BlueDriver at $99.95 and the OBDLink MX+ at $139.95 cost the same in year five as they did on day one, because neither charges a subscription. The cheapest sticker price on this page can be the most expensive scanner you own. Add the years before you add the cart.
  4. Whether it works on your phone at all. An iPhone cannot pair with a Bluetooth Classic adapter — the radio is the wrong kind, and no app fixes that. A Classic-only dongle that works fine on Android is a return on an iPhone. BlueDriver, the OBDLink MX+ and the Veepeak BLE+ all work on iPhone; the Veepeak does it through Bluetooth Low Energy, which is the whole reason the "BLE" is in its name. Check the Bluetooth type before you check the price.
  5. What it draws while it sits plugged in. The dongle lives in the port under your steering column, and a scanner left plugged in draws power whether you are driving or not. The OBDLink MX+ publishes a 2 mA low-power mode and backs it with a 3-year warranty; Veepeak publishes no sleep mode and still recommends unplugging between uses, and FIXD's own guidance is the same. A scanner that quietly drains the battery it is supposed to help you protect is solving the wrong problem. If the maker does not publish a sleep figure, assume you will be unplugging it.
