Where the money actually goes

Most people price a flat tire at the tow bill and stop there. The tow bill is usually the smallest honest number in the night, because it is the only one that arrives as a receipt.

The tow or call-out

A local tow commonly runs $75–$150, and that is before mileage beyond the included radius, after-hours surcharges, or a second truck when the first cannot take the vehicle. Roadside assistance memberships soften this — until the one night the membership does not cover the distance, the vehicle, or the hour.

The wait

Two hours is a kind figure. A truck dispatched across town at rush hour or at 1 AM takes longer, and every minute of it is spent on a shoulder, in a parking lot, or in a car that cannot move. Priced at even a modest hourly value, the wait alone often matches the tow bill.

The missed everything else

The shift that starts without you, the gig that goes to another driver, the appointment with a no-show fee, the ride home you buy because the car is on a flatbed — none of these appear on the tow receipt, and all of them are real. This is why two drivers can have the same flat tire and wildly different bills: the tire is the same, the night is not.

The kit does not make flat tires free. It makes the common ones — the slow leak, the cold-morning drop, the tire a few PSI down — a ten-minute stop instead of a night.

FAQ

Is the tow range $75–$150 realistic everywhere?

It is a typical range for a short local tow in the US, not a quote. Rural distances, after-hours calls, and heavy vehicles all push it higher — which is why the calculator lets you type your own number instead of trusting ours.

Does the kit really replace a tow?

Only for the common cases: slow leaks, cold-weather pressure drops, and tires a few PSI down. A cut, a blown bead, or a tire going flat in minutes still needs a shop or a tow. The honest claim is narrower and stronger: most stranded nights start as a pressure problem, and pressure problems are the ones a kit solves.

Why count my time as money?

Because the wait is the part nobody reimburses. Even if your job pays you anyway, two hours on a shoulder are two hours you do not get back — and for hourly, gig, and shift workers, they are literally unpaid. The calculator lets you set that value to $0 if you prefer; the tow and ride costs alone usually still clear the kit's price.

What if I already have roadside assistance?

Then your tow line may be $0 — run the calculator that way. The wait, the missed shift, and the ruined evening remain, and they are the reason many members still carry a kit: assistance gets you rescued, a kit gets you moving.

How accurate is the "you keep" number?

It is exactly as accurate as the numbers you typed. It is not a promise, a rebate, or a savings guarantee — it is your inputs minus $49.99. Change any input and it changes with it, which is the point.
