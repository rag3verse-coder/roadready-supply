Frequently asked questions

How do I keep the kit charged between delivery shifts?

Charge it at home overnight like a phone, and keep it cased in the trunk. If you forget and the battery is dead mid-route, the 12V car socket powers the same top-off — the dual-power setup exists so a dead battery never strands a shift.

What should I do if a tire goes soft in the middle of a route?

Pull fully off the road, hazards on, and bring the tire back to the door-jamb number with the auto-stop set. If it holds, finish the route and check it again before the next shift. If it keeps dropping, that tire goes to a shop — do not run a full shift topping off the same tire.

Does delivery driving really wear tires faster than commuting?

Yes — constant stop-start, heavy loads, and curb contact wear tires faster and leak air sooner than a commuter's pattern. That is why a weekly cold check is a money habit for a driver, not a chore: correct pressure means better mileage, longer tire life, and fewer blowout risks on a loaded run.

What pressure do I use when the car is loaded with orders?

Use the number on the driver's door-jamb sticker. Some stickers list a separate figure for a fully loaded car — if yours does, use the loaded figure when the car is loaded and the standard figure when it is not. Never use the tire sidewall number; that is a maximum, not a target.
