Delivery driving is tire driving. Hundreds of stops, curbs, potholes, and hot asphalt in a shift, and every minute at a gas station with a broken air pump is a minute not delivering. A trunk kit turns a low-pressure warning into a two-minute top-off between stops instead of a lost shift. Check at the start of the shift, not when the warning light forces you to, and keep the number for your car written on the case.

Why delivery wears tires differently

Constant stop-start, heavy loads, and curb contact wear tires faster and leak air sooner than a commuter's pattern. That makes a weekly cold check a money habit, not a chore: correct pressure means better mileage, longer tire life, and fewer blowout risks on a loaded run.

The route routine

  1. Morning cold check, one minute.
  2. Top off to the door-jamb number, cutoff does the stopping.
  3. If a tire keeps dropping shift after shift, that is a shop visit, not a daily top-off.
